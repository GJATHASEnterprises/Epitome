# Epitome Step — Finishing Guide

Two finishing processes: one for Walnut step faces, one for Obsidian ABS faces.

---

## Walnut finishing — Rubio Monocoat

Rubio Monocoat Pure is a single-coat plant-based wood oil. One coat fully saturates the walnut. Do not apply two coats.

### What you need
- Rubio Monocoat Pure (Oil Plus 2C is acceptable but unnecessary for small parts)
- Lint-free cloths ×3 (application, spread, buff)
- 180-grit sandpaper
- 220-grit sandpaper
- Clean flat surface to work on

### Process

**Step 1 — Sanding**
1. Sand all walnut faces and step surfaces with 180-grit, following the grain direction
2. Do not cross-grain sand — this leaves scratches visible after oiling
3. Follow with 220-grit in the same direction
4. Wipe clean with a dry cloth; remove all dust
5. Optional: wipe with a barely-damp cloth and let dry 10 minutes — this raises grain for a smoother final surface. Sand again lightly with 220 grit after.

**Step 2 — Application**
1. Apply a pea-sized amount of Rubio Monocoat Pure to a lint-free cloth
2. Rub into the walnut surface with circular or grain-following strokes
3. Work the oil in thoroughly — it should look wet and dark
4. Continue until all surfaces are coated
5. **Remove excess immediately** — do not let oil pool or dry on surface
6. Use a clean cloth to wipe off all excess, leaving a thin even film

**Step 3 — Cure**
1. Leave parts flat at room temperature (20°C+) for 1 hour
2. After 1 hour, lightly buff with a clean dry cloth — this brings up the satin sheen
3. Full hardness in 24 hours — do not assemble until cured
4. Do not stack oiled parts during cure; lay flat with air circulation

### Tips for school makerspace
- Use a plastic sheet or parchment paper as your work surface — oil can stain benches
- Rubio Monocoat cloths are self-heating — roll them flat and wet before disposing, or soak in water. Dry bundled oily cloths can smoulder.
- A 100 mL tin of Rubio Monocoat Pure does approximately 20 step sets (60 faces) — buy one tin for Batch 1
- Warm walnut absorbs oil better — if workshop is cold, warm parts with a hairdryer for 30 seconds before applying

## Obsidian finishing — Matte black ABS

ABS laser-cut parts have visible cut edges and often minor surface imperfections. The goal is a uniform matte black finish with no visible layer lines or edge burn.

### What you need
- Grey sandable primer spray (Rust-Oleum or equivalent)
- Matte black spray paint (Rust-Oleum 2X Ultra Cover Matte or equivalent)
- 220-grit wet/dry sandpaper
- 320-grit wet/dry sandpaper
- Isopropyl alcohol (IPA) wipes
- Latex gloves
- Cardboard or hanging wire as spray surface

### Process

**Step 1 — Prep**
1. Sand all faces with 220-grit to remove laser burn marks and scorch lines from cut edges
2. Focus on edges — run sandpaper along each cut edge at 45° to break any sharp slag
3. Wipe all surfaces with IPA to remove dust, oils, and finger marks
4. Allow to dry 5 minutes
5. **Do not touch surfaces after IPA wipe** — skin oils prevent paint adhesion

**Step 2 — Primer**
1. In a well-ventilated area (outdoors or ventilated spray booth)
2. Hold part 20–25 cm from nozzle
3. Apply thin, even primer coat — do not build up; one pass per face
4. Allow 30 minutes dry time at room temperature
5. Lightly sand with 320-grit once dry — this creates tooth for the colour coat
6. Wipe dust off with a dry cloth

**Step 3 — Matte black coat**
1. Apply matte black spray, thin coats (2–3 passes per face)
2. Keep the can moving — don't dwell; runs are difficult to fix on matte
3. Allow 1 hour between coats if applying multiple
4. Minimum 2 coats for full even coverage
5. Final cure: 24 hours before handling or assembling

**Step 4 — Inspection**
- Check in bright light for any missed spots, orange peel texture, or runs
- If runs: let cure 24 hours, sand with 400-grit, re-spray
- If thin spots: re-spray after 1 hour

### Tips for school makerspace
- Spray outdoors or in the spray booth — ABS primer and paint fumes are significant
- A shared can of Rust-Oleum 2X Black covers approximately 20 step sets
- Hang parts on a bent wire hook through the rear M3 hole if available — this keeps faces clean while spraying edges
- Cold temperature slows cure time — if workshop is under 15°C, move parts to a warm room to cure
- Matte finishes show fingerprints more than gloss — handle with gloves until assembled
